# F0.6. Comparison with the private corpus

[← F0](../F0-leaks-and-gate.md)

## Why

A list of names will not catch a fragment with the names removed, or a paragraph rewritten in other words. This layer checks whether text meant for publication is similar to anything in client material, whatever names it uses.

## What to do

1. On K12, inside the private network, a small service with a single endpoint: it takes text and returns `similar: true/false`, the highest score and a rule id. It never returns content from the private corpus.
2. Two checks. Literal match: a MinHash index of shingles (8 characters and 6 words) built from the private corpus. Semantic match: paragraph embeddings of the input text compared with paragraph embeddings of the private corpus (the bge-m3 model already runs locally).
3. Calibrate thresholds on two sets, both kept private: paragraphs from the private corpus rewritten by a local model (these must be caught) and public texts on similar topics (these should pass). Set the threshold so that no rewritten paragraph passes, and record how many public texts are stopped at that threshold.
4. The publisher calls the service for every text file.

## Done when

No rewritten paragraph from the calibration set passes. The false alarm rate on public texts is measured and recorded. Public documentation describes the method and the overall results; a private report holds the details.

## Notes

The service has read access to the indexes only, not to the private database. Queries are not logged together with their content.

## Progress

- 2026-09-28: calibration on a clean set. The negatives are the published documents and arXiv paper summaries, which we removed from the private corpus because they are public. The engine documentation was dropped as a negative because it was written from notes that are in the corpus. The literal layer separates the two sets completely (threshold 0.4175, no misses, no false alarms). The semantic layer does not: at 5% false alarms it lets 12% of rewritten paragraphs through, and the calibrated threshold of 0.8723 would hold 21 of the 100 published files. The condition "no rewritten paragraph passes" cannot be met with this method. Until a decision, an operational threshold of 0.93 is in force, which holds none of the published files.
- 2026-09-28, afternoon: we tested whether relative measures improve the semantic layer. Hypothesis: a paraphrase stands out against one private note, while a text that is merely on the same topic is close to many, so the gap between the nearest and the next neighbours (margin) or similarity corrected for the density of the neighbourhood (CSLS) will separate the two sets better. Result at 5% false alarms: plain similarity lets 15% of paraphrases through, margin 39%, CSLS 35%, and on the half held out for checking 19%, 37% and 32% respectively. The relative measures would also hold more published files (24 and 33 of 100, against 13 for plain similarity). Hypothesis rejected; plain similarity and the operational threshold of 0.93 stay. Next candidate: a two-stage check in which paragraphs above a candidate threshold are judged by a local model against the nearest private notes.
- 2026-09-28, evening: two-stage check. Stage one (similarity of at least 0.8137) passes 98% of paraphrases and 97% of public texts to stage two, which is a local model judging whether the paragraph restates one of the three nearest private notes. Result: 17% of paraphrases let through, 12% false alarms, 33 of 100 published files held (668 model judgements). This is worse than the plain threshold, so the two-stage check was not switched on. An analysis of nearest neighbours shows why: our documents are closest to notes about Exocortex itself (architecture, backlog), and paper summaries are closest to newsletter summaries in the corpus. The model rightly sees the same content in them, only it is not client material. The next step is the owner's decision on which parts of the corpus are to be protected.
- 2026-09-28, late evening: the owner narrowed the protected corpus to client and private material. Notes about Exocortex itself and content from public sources (news, newsletters, RSS) were excluded. The index now has 47,711 paragraphs. Result of the plain measure at 5% false alarms: 5.8% of paraphrases let through (4.2% on the held-out half), threshold 0.8584. The literal layer still separates the two sets completely (threshold 0.3699). Two-stage check: 12.6% of paraphrases let through at 2.5% false alarms, so the plain measure wins. At the 0.8584 threshold, 36 of 100 published files would be held, mostly because of closeness to client material and private product material. These files went onto a private list for review before the repository is switched. Until the review, the operational threshold of 0.93 is in force.
