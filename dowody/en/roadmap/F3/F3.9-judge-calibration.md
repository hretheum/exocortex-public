# F3.9. Pilot: calibrating the automatic judge

[← F3](../F3-first-pass.md)

> **Status: to do** · as of 30 September 2026
>
> The task depends on F3.8, which is "to do", so it cannot be started yet. The judge model has not been chosen and there are no calibration results.

## In brief

Human rating is slow and cannot be done for the whole corpus. So we check whether a second language model, from a different family than the one extracting claims, can find errors: how many it catches and how often it raises a false alarm. We compare it with the human ratings on the same 120 claims.

## Why

Human rating does not scale to the whole corpus. A model from a different family than the extractor can serve as a screen, a first sieve that points to claims worth reviewing, but only once we know how many errors it catches and how many false alarms it raises. We choose a model from a different family because models of the same family may share weaknesses. Without this check such a model could let errors through or mark correct claims as wrong.

## What to do

1. Choose a judge model from a different family than the extracting models.
2. Run it on the same 120 claims with a prompt aimed at finding errors.
3. Compare with the human ratings: sensitivity (the share of errors of a class that were detected) for each error class from F3.8, and the share of correct claims marked as wrong.
4. Record the conclusion: can the judge decide, can it only route claims to review, or is it of no use.

## Done when

The calibration results in both languages are in the experiment folder, with numbers and a conclusion on acceptable use of the judge.
