# F3.5. Blind sample and rating

[← F3](../F3-first-pass.md)

> **Status: done** · as of 30 September 2026
>
> The tool is in `exocortex/lab/blind.py` (commands `exocortex lab blind draw`, `import`, `summary` and `publish`) and passed a test on the toy experiment, a trial experiment used to check tools: it drew 24 items and 2 repeats, the rating page does not show the configuration, and ratings entered by hand (test ratings) reached the `exp_judgments` table. The confidence intervals agree with computations in other tools: Wilson intervals with statsmodels to 15 decimal places, and the bootstrap of the difference between configurations with numpy on 100 000 replicates (0.167 [0.000; 0.417] in both). Rating of real claims from the "intent or fact" experiment has not started yet; rating in the owner's interface is a separate task (F2.10), also done.

## In brief

For a human rating to be fair, the rater must not know which variant of the program produced a given claim. The tool draws claims, shows them in random order with no information about the variant, then collects the ratings and computes the result together with a confidence interval, the range in which the true result probably lies.

## Why

The main result of the experiment needs a human rating: only a person can decide whether a claim presents a plan as a fact. If the rater knew which variant a claim came from, they might, even unintentionally, rate one of them more leniently. That is why the ratings are blind, and why we give the result with a confidence interval, so that it is visible how far it can be trusted with a small sample.

## What to do

1. Draw a sample of claims from the experiment results, stratified by configuration and document length, with a recorded seed.
2. A rating page in Obsidian, in the experiment folder: for each claim its text, the quote and the part of the document around the quote, without the configuration name, in random order. Below each, checkboxes: correct, mode swap, distorted number or name, other error, and space for a comment.
3. A script that reads the page after rating, joins the ratings with configurations and stores them in `exp_judgments`.
4. Confidence intervals: the Wilson method for proportions, bootstrap over documents for differences between configurations.
5. The rating page is published only after rating is finished, together with the results.

## Done when

On the toy experiment the tool generates the page, ratings entered by hand end up in the table, and the confidence intervals match a check computation in a different tool.
