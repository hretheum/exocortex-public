# F3.5. Blind sample and rating

[← F3](../F3-first-pass.md)

## Why

The deciding metric needs a human rating. The rater must not know which configuration a claim comes from, and the result needs a confidence interval.

## What to do

1. Draw a sample of claims from the experiment results, stratified by configuration and document length, with a recorded seed.
2. A rating page in Obsidian, in the experiment folder: for each claim its text, the quote and the part of the document around the quote, without the configuration name, in random order. Below each, checkboxes: correct, mode swap, distorted number or name, other error, and space for a comment.
3. A script that reads the page after rating, joins the ratings with configurations and stores them in `exp_judgments`.
4. Confidence intervals: the Wilson method for proportions, bootstrap over documents for differences between configurations.
5. The rating page is published only after rating is finished, together with the results.

## Done when

On the toy experiment the tool generates the page, ratings entered by hand end up in the table, and the confidence intervals match a check computation in a different tool.

## Progress

- 2026-09-29: the tool is in `exocortex/lab/blind.py` (commands `exocortex lab blind draw`, `import`, `summary` and `publish`). On the toy experiment it drew 24 items and 2 repeats, and the rating page does not show the configuration. Ratings entered by hand (a test of the tool, not a judgement of content) reached the `exp_judgments` table. The Wilson intervals agree with the statsmodels package to 15 decimal places, and the bootstrap interval of the difference between configurations with an independent computation in numpy on 100 000 replicates: 0.167 [0.000; 0.417] in both. The working page has `publish: false` in its header, so the publisher does not send it; after rating, the results go to a separate page with the configuration names.
