# F2.10. Blind rating in the owner's interface

[← F2](../F2-lab.md)

> **Status: done** · as of 30 September 2026
>
> The owner's interface (a panel where the project owner approves drafts and rates results) has a "Blind rating" screen: one claim at a time, with its quote, without the configuration name and in random order. On 30 September 2026 all items of the toy experiment (a trial experiment that tests the whole path) were rated on it (4 claims and 2 repeats, test ratings by the agent, not a rating of the content), and the results and confidence intervals (ranges in which the true value probably lies) are identical to loading the same ratings from a page filled in by hand. Neither the screen nor the server's answers contain configurations or partial results, and the page in Obsidian stays as a fallback.

## In brief

This task gives the project owner a convenient screen for rating experiment results without knowing which setting a given answer came from. Every rating is saved at once, and after a break the rater can return exactly where they stopped.

## Why

The result of an experiment depends on a human judgment: someone has to decide whether the model's answer is correct. This is done blind, because a rater who knows which setting produced an answer judges it through their own expectations. The current rating page in Obsidian (the notebook app where we keep our documents) works, but with more than a hundred claims it is awkward and easy to get wrong. The new screen shows one claim at a time, in random order, so rating the first experiment and the later ones is faster and less prone to mistakes.

## What to do

1. A rating screen in the owner's interface: one claim at a time, its quote and the passage around the quote, without the configuration name, in random order. Buttons for the categories (correct, mode swap, that is, a plan or guess stated as fact, garbled number or name, other error), a comment field, a jump to the next item after rating, keyboard shortcuts and resuming after a break.
2. Ratings go to the `exp_judgments` table through the same validation code as the import from the page, including the repeated items (the same claims shown a second time to check whether the rater is consistent).
3. A progress view (how many rated, how many left), without partial results, so they cannot influence later ratings.
4. The page in Obsidian stays as a fallback.

## Done when

On the toy experiment all items are rated in the interface, and the results and confidence intervals are the same as when loaded from the page. The rater sees neither configurations nor partial results.
