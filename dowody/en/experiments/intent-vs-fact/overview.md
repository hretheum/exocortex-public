# Intent or fact

## Abstract

Language models often describe what has only been intended as if it were done. In the abstracts of scientific papers, facts (what was measured) sit next to announcements and hypotheses (what we propose, what might work), so they are a good place to test whether we can tell the two apart. This experiment asks two questions: does extracting claims from text with a mandatory mode field confuse a plan with a fact less often than extraction without it, and do our own summaries turn an intention into a fact.

As of 28 September 2026: the corpus is ready (2478 papers from arXiv), the hypothesis card does not exist yet and nothing has been measured. This page describes the preparation, not a result.

## Question and hypothesis

The general question: can we reliably tell apart, in a text, sentences about what is a fact from sentences about what is a plan, a requirement, a goal or a hypothesis, and do our summaries keep that distinction.

The working hypothesis from the roadmap (not frozen yet):

- H1: adding a mandatory mode field (fact, plan, requirement, hypothesis) to the extraction schema reduces the share of claims that present a plan, a requirement or a goal as an accomplished fact.

- H0: the difference between the variant with and without the mode field is smaller than the threshold written in the card, or the confidence interval includes zero.

The final wording, including the threshold, will be set in the hypothesis card ([F3.4](../../roadmap/F3/F3.4-hypothesis-card.md)) and frozen before any measurement.

## Preregistration

The hypothesis card has not been written yet, so there is no checksum and no entry in the preregistration register. The rule is this: the card is frozen and published before the extractor is run on the corpus even once, apart from five test documents. The register entry is dated earlier than the first run, and the verification script confirms the checksum.

Below are the working assumptions from the roadmap. They may change until the card is frozen, and after that only a new version of the card changes them.

| Role | Metric | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| deciding | share of claims with a mode swap in a blind sample rated by a person | to be set in the card before measurement |
| guard | share of fragments without a valid model answer | no more than 5% |
| guard | number of useful claims per document | no less than 70% of the baseline |

The baseline is the same extractor without the mode field, with the same model and on the same documents. Working sample sizes: tuning 20 documents, a control set of 10 documents and a blind sample for the pilot of 120 claims, all drawn with a recorded seed and stratified by document length.

## Data

The source is the abstracts of arXiv papers that our engine downloaded and summarised in August and September 2026, together with the Polish summaries and findings generated by the engine. We downloaded the abstracts again from the arXiv API so that the corpus does not depend on what the engine stored. We do not use the full texts of the papers.

After exclusions the frame has 2478 papers: 602 in the low stratum (relevance score 1 to 4), 1005 in the middle (5 to 7) and 871 in the high (8 to 10). Eight items dropped out: one duplicate, two abstracts shorter than 50 words and five papers held by the publishing gate because they contain a name from its private list. The ids of those five stay private, because they would point at the names.

Basis for use: the abstracts are arXiv metadata under CC0 1.0, and the summaries are a product of our engine, so we publish them in full. The full description of the corpus, the legal basis and the sampling frame is in the document [Experiment corpus](corpus.md). The files to download are below.

## Method

The experiment goes through the whole cycle, at two scales. The quick test (scale S) compares both variants of the extractor on the tuning sample and, only after that has been rated, on the control set, which we open once. The pilot (scale M) extends the comparison to a matrix of 4 to 8 configurations: two local models of different families, a schema with and without the mode field, and processing whole documents or fragments.

The rating is blind: a person sees the claim, the quote and the passage of the document, but does not know which configuration the claim comes from, and the order is random. We compute confidence intervals with the Wilson method for proportions and a bootstrap over documents for differences between configurations. At the end of the pilot we calibrate an automatic judge from a different model family and check how many errors it catches and how many false alarms it gives.

The order of tasks: the corpus ([F3.1](../../roadmap/F3/F3.1-public-corpus-selection.md), [F3.2](../../roadmap/F3/F3.2-corpus-manifest.md)), the claim extractor, the hypothesis card, the blind sample tool, the quick test with gate G1, the pilot and gate G2 with the report.

## How to reproduce

Today the construction of the corpus can be repeated. The corpus files and checksums are available in the data section, and the list of ids is in the manifest file.

1. Download `manifest.csv` and `corpus.jsonl` and compare their SHA-256 sums with those given on this page.

2. For any id from the manifest, download the abstract from the arXiv API (at most one request every three seconds), normalise the whitespace and compute SHA-256. The result should match the `abstract_sha256` column, unless arXiv has published a newer version of the paper since.

3. The summaries are available only in the corpus file, because they were written earlier in the Exocortex engine. Their sums can be checked in the `summary_sha256` column.

The script that builds the corpus is `lab/corpus/intent_vs_fact.py` in the repository. Repeating the measurements will be possible once the card is frozen and the first runs are done.

## Limitations

The relevance score reflects the interests of the lab’s owner, not the quality of a paper. The strata only make sure that every sample has papers from different parts of the scale.

The frame is the papers the engine downloaded over two months, not a random sample of all of arXiv, so the results say nothing about arXiv as a whole.

The abstracts are in English and the summaries in Polish, so the comparison of intent and fact goes across languages. The engine did not record which model wrote each summary.

Until a second expert joins (F6.1), the blind sample is rated by one person, so agreement between raters cannot be computed yet.

The method will not detect completeness (whether the most important claims were extracted), interpretation errors with a correct quote, or weaknesses shared by the extracting and the rating model.
